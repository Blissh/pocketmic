Windows Integration

What Micstream does
-------------------

Micstream installs a tiny driver that makes your phone appear like a **standalone microphone** on Windows — recognized by Zoom, Discord, OBS, or any other Windows application.

It also **removes background noise**: DeepFilterNet, an AI noise-cancellation model, runs on your PC and filters out
keyboard clicks, fans, and room noise. It's on by default and free.

Have Questions?

Frequently Asked Questions
--------------------------

### Which versions of Windows are supported?

Micstream supports Windows 10 and Windows 11.

### Does Micstream work with macOS or Linux?

Linux is supported: the client registers a PipeWire virtual microphone — no driver needed — on any current distribution (Ubuntu, Debian, Fedora, Arch). The macOS client is in development.

[Read the Linux setup guide →](./guides/use-phone-as-mic-linux)

### Is Micstream available on iOS?

Yes. The iPhone app is on the App Store and needs iOS 18 or newer. It connects over Wi-Fi; a USB connection is currently Android-only.

[Read the iPhone setup guide →](./guides/use-iphone-as-pc-mic)

### Does it support all Android phones?

Yes. Any modern Android device will work. Flagship phones usually provide better audio quality.

### Why does Micstream need to install a driver?

To make Windows treat your phone like a hardware microphone, Micstream needs to create an actual microphone device. Our driver does exactly that — it creates a virtual mic device that seamlessly integrates with apps like Discord, Zoom, Google Meet or OBS.

### Will the driver interfere with other audio devices?

No. The driver runs alongside your existing audio devices without affecting them. Micstream simply adds a new, virtual microphone to your system.

### How is latency via USB vs. Wi-Fi?

USB provides ultra-low latency—often under 10ms on high-end devices. Wi-Fi performance varies based on signal strength and network conditions, so latency may be higher.

[Read the full setup guide →](./guides/use-phone-as-mic-windows)

### Is the audio suitable for professional streaming and recording?

Yes, depending on your phone. Many phones have built-in microphones that already beat budget USB mics. Most USB mics around $70 record in 16-bit, while flagship phones often record in 24-bit. Micstream never downsamples the audio and hands it to Windows as 32-bit float at 48 kHz, so you can get professional-quality sound.

[See the OBS setup guide →](./guides/phone-mic-for-obs)

### What sampling rate/bit depth does Micstream use?

Micstream delivers the audio to Windows as 32-bit floating point at 48 kHz. It is never downsampled.

### Does Micstream remove background noise?

Yes. The Windows and Linux clients include DeepFilterNet, an AI noise-cancellation model that filters out keyboard clicks, fans, and room noise before the audio reaches your apps. It runs on your computer, not your phone, is on by default and free, and you can switch it on or off from the phone app while streaming.

### Can I adjust audio settings like gain?

Yes. You can adjust your device's volume settings, effectively controlling gain. Future updates may offer more advanced audio controls.

### Does the Windows client or app store/capture any audio data or personal data?

No audio content is ever transmitted to our servers or stored — all audio stays between your devices on your local network. The software sends pseudonymous reliability diagnostics (version info, connection quality, error codes) to our own EU servers to help us detect and fix issues. No personal data, usernames, file paths, or audio is included. See our Privacy Policy for details.

### How do I select Micstream in other applications?

Just choose "Micstream" in your application's audio or microphone settings. Most apps automatically recognize it as a valid input device.

### Can I use my phone normally while streaming audio?

Yes. You can still use your phone for other tasks while it's serving as your PC mic. Our app runs in the background without disrupting regular phone usage.
